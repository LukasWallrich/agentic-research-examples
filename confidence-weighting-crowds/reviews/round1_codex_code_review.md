## Answer

**The preregistered empirical and model-reproduction claims are not supported as presented.** The current outputs report **81 datasets**, while parts of the [manuscript](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/manuscript/manuscript.md:21) still say 80. There is no README in the project; the [decision log](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/decision_log.md:27) records intentions, not results. I made no edits.

Findings, ordered by severity:

1. **H6 is marked supported by changing its outcome and success rule.** The [preregistration](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/prereg/preregistration.md:117) requires at least 60% agreement on the sign of the dataset’s crowd-accuracy gap. The current [H6 output](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/output/h6.json) gives **30/81 (37%)**: H6 fails. [h6.py](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/h6.py:43) instead declares success if *either* that outcome or the later-added Brier outcome reaches 60%. Its Brier result, **72/81 (89%)**, provides no demonstrated predictive discrimination: every dataset is assigned a cell predicting a negative Brier effect, and exactly 72 datasets have a negative observed effect. Predicting “negative” for all 81 gives the same score. This outcome-contingent H6 change is not recorded in [deviations.md](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/deviations.md:13).

2. **The empirical group draws are not judge subsets.** [empirical.py](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/empirical.py:135) uses `rng.integers`, sampling judges **with replacement**. A size-three draw from exactly three judges contains three distinct judges only 22% of the time. Repeated judges alter both EW and CW and invalidate the stated group-size interpretation. The code also runs an \(m\) whenever *any* item has enough judges, rather than limiting \(m\) to the dataset’s **median** judges per item as preregistered. The 64-item cap is sampled once per \(m\), although [V3](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/deviations.md:21) says “per draw.” The seed uses Python’s process-randomized `hash(name)`, so the fixed-seed reproduction claim does not hold across ordinary runs.

3. **The added Brier outcome answers a different question.** The [leave-one-judge-out code](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/empirical.py:161) correctly removes the target judge from both estimates, and **positive** `brier_improvement` correctly means CW has the lower Brier score. But it predicts another judge’s *correctness* from the other judges’ correctness; it does not score the crowd’s estimate of item truth. A better-weighted crowd can therefore make a worse prediction of an average held-out judge. Calling it a continuous version of the preregistered crowd-accuracy test is misleading. Its post-outcome addition is disclosed in V2, but [V2’s claim](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/deviations.md:42) that the preregistered result is “consistent in direction” is false: current [empirical results](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/output/empirical_meta.json) show mean crowd-accuracy **CW−EW = +0.00310** (95% bootstrap CI **[+0.00034, +0.00707]**), versus mean Brier improvement **−0.000805** (CI **[−0.001307, −0.000428]**).

4. **The simulation is substantially different from the registered design.** [simulate.py](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/simulate.py:36) has one 40,000-item realization per cell, not 200 repetitions; omits \(m=4\); changes the \(\tau\) grid; and uses one confidence-noise level rather than two. These changes are not listed in [deviations.md](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/deviations.md:5). Its supposed persistent judge ability and overconfidence traits are redrawn for every item (`(N_ITEMS, MMAX)`), and the additive “ability” term acts as a response-direction shift. More seriously, simulated confidence depends directly on the **true answer**, \(T_i s_{ij}\): an information advantage unavailable to a judge. V1 records replacing \(\kappa\) with \(\phi\), but its rationale is also mathematically unsound: an item-common shift inside a nonlinear confidence function can change judges’ *relative* weights and hence the CW decision.

5. **The prespecified moderation and simulation summaries are overstated.** H5 specified the **crowd-accuracy** gap and predicted a negative \(\rho\) coefficient. The fitted coefficient is **+0.133 (SE 0.058)**, the opposite sign; the negative coefficient highlighted in the [manuscript](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/manuscript/manuscript.md:91) belongs to the added Brier outcome. For H2, [sim_summary.json](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/output/sim_summary.json) records **51 of 384** group-size steps decreasing by more than the stated 0.002 tolerance, across 18 cells; the largest drop is about 0.0175. That does not support the stated non-decreasing hypothesis. H1 does find five cells with a negative crowd-accuracy gap. H3 finds interior grid optima in 16 cells, but [\(\lambda^\star\)](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/simulate.py:95) is selected and evaluated on the same items, without uncertainty or independent validation.

6. **The psychometric estimates and pooling do not implement the promised inference.** The unequal-group ICC formula is recognizable, but [empirical.py](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/empirical.py:99) applies it to **trials**, not the judge–item table claimed in V5; repeated trials from one judge count as separate judges’ observations. The logistic likelihood is conventional, but its optimizer result is accepted without a convergence check and the preregistered subject-clustered bootstrap SE is absent. The [meta-analysis](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/empirical.py:239) is an unweighted dataset mean with a dataset bootstrap, **not a random-effects meta-analysis**. The promised clipped-confidence robustness analysis is absent. Basic numeric accuracy coercion, dataset-level confidence normalization, and judge-level mean vote/confidence aggregation otherwise follow the stated definitions.

7. **The model-to-data match and reporting contain internal inconsistencies.** [h6.py](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/h6.py:23) standardizes the *raw* slope despite describing “log calibration slope,” and matches on \(\beta,\rho\) while omitting confidence dispersion and group size. **70/81** empirical \(\rho\) values fall below the smallest simulated \(\rho\); **64/81** datasets map to one cell. Simulation Brier arithmetic uses the same target-exclusion and sign convention as the empirical code, but averages over fixed simulated group sizes while the empirical Brier score uses all available judges per item. The manuscript’s claim that Brier improvement is negative “in all 48 cells” confuses [the summary’s minimum over group sizes](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/analysis/scripts/simulate.py:127) with the cell mean: **three cell means are positive**. The promised formal gap derivation is also absent from the [manuscript’s finite-sample section](/Users/lukaswallrich/Documents/Coding/ai-jdm-paper4/manuscript/manuscript.md:25), which gives a verbal bias–variance argument. Finally, the claim that weighting votes by raw confidence is Bayes-optimal under independence is too strong; the usual independent-vote likelihood rule weights by log odds of correctness, under its assumptions.

**What checks out:** the empirical and simulated Brier calculations both exclude the target judge in their normal path, and both interpret `Brier(EW) − Brier(CW)` with the correct sign. The reported H1 negative cells and the arithmetic in the current JSON summaries agree with their CSV inputs. Those checks do not rescue the preregistered H5/H6 conclusions or the group-subsampling error.
