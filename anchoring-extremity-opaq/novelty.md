# Novelty: the five closest papers and what this study adds

Search basis (2026-09-27; Imhoff & Barker, 2023, added on 2026-09-28 after the published version of one source study was found): OpenAlex, Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar citers of the OpAQ data paper (24 works) and of the OpAQ meta-analysis preprint; OpenAlex citers since 2022 of Lieder et al. (2018a; 164), Lieder et al. (2018b; 21) and Turner & Schley (2016; 54); keyword and web searches for computational models of anchoring and for anchor extremity. Full reports are in `scoping/`. DOIs were checked against Crossref. No work was found that relates the proportional anchoring index to anchor extremity across many items with trial-level data, or that compares process models on OpAQ.

| # | Paper | What it did | What this study adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Röseler, L., & Schütz, A. (2022). Hanging the anchor off a new ship: A meta-analysis of anchoring effects. PsyArXiv. https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/wf2tn | Meta-analysis of the OpAQ data (90 studies, k = 386 items, N = 17,708). Hedges' g rose with the mean relative distance between anchor and true value (b = 0.040 per unit, k = 312). Concluded against the prediction that extreme anchors give smaller effects, and called for a unitless "relative strength of adjustment" metric to formalise how adjustment depends on anchor distance. | g is a standardised mean difference, so it rises with anchor distance even when anchors pull estimates by a constant proportion. We measure the proportional pull directly (log-scale elasticity and anchoring index), separate within-item from across-item variation, and test the pattern against pre-computed predictions of five process models. |
| 2 | Schley, D. R., & Weingarten, E. (2026). Fifty years of anchoring effects: A theoretical reintegration and meta-analysis. Management Science (online 2026-05-19). https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03238 | Meta-analysis of 1,280 high-vs-low effect sizes (482 studies). Extremity was a binary flag (49 of 1,280 effect sizes "extreme"); coefficient -0.02 [-0.20, 0.16]. An earlier version added a meta-study with 8 items and anchors at calibration percentiles; the high-minus-low gap in z-scored estimates grew with extremity. Theories were compared qualitatively; the authors call for formal models. | Continuous, outcome-free extremity (log distance of anchor from truth) instead of a binary author label; a proportional outcome instead of g or z-scores; trial-level data from many studies; formal models whose predictions are simulated on the actual designs and frozen before the data are read. |
| 3 | Barrera-Lemarchand, F., Balenzuela, P., Bahrami, B., Deroy, O., & Navajas, J. (2024). Promoting erroneous divergent opinions increases the wisdom of crowds. Psychological Science, 35, 872-886. https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976241252138 | Modelled the anchored crowd mean as a weighted average of anchor and crowd mean with weight w0·exp(-beta·|A - truth|). In 30 percentage questions with anchors fixed at 5% and 95% (N = 396), the anchoring weight fell with anchor distance (r = -.56). | Their distance varied only through the true value on a bounded 0-100 scale, which can compress responses mechanically. We use open-ended ratio-scale quantities, vary the anchor itself within items (up to 20 anchor levels, and continuous random anchors), and compare this discounting pattern with rival models that predict rising or constant proportional influence. |
| 4 | Lieder, F., Griffiths, T. L., Huys, Q. J. M., & Goodman, N. D. (2018). The anchoring bias reflects rational use of cognitive resources. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 25, 322-349. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-017-1286-8 | Resource-rational anchoring-and-adjustment: a sampling chain starts at the anchor and stops after the number of adjustments that balances error and time cost. Fitted Jacowitz & Kahneman's (1995) anchoring index for 15 quantities (predicted 53.2% vs observed 48.5%). Stated that bias grows in proportion to anchor distance for a fixed number of adjustments; did not derive or test how the proportional index changes with extremity. | We derive the extremity prediction for two variants: a fixed number of adjustments (proportional pull rises with extremity) and an adaptive, resource-rational number (proportional pull falls). We test both on data with many anchor levels per item. |
| 5 | Imhoff, R., & Barker, P. (2023). Connecting the dots: Nonlinear patterns in the presence of symbolic and nonsymbolic numerical standards. Judgment and Decision Making, 18, e34. https://doi.org/10.1017/jdm.2023.31 | Study 2 (N = 803): participants estimated dot patterns of about 500 dots next to standards that differed by up to 400 dots in 20-dot steps, each labelled with its exact number (a symbolic anchor). Estimates assimilated to the anchor, with a cubic pattern that levelled off beyond a difference of about 300 dots. This is the closest precedent for a within-item extremity function with many anchor levels. | Their evidence covers one perceptual quantity (dot numerosity) with anchors between about 0.2 and 1.8 times the target, on a z-scored raw scale. We test the shape for general-knowledge magnitudes with anchors up to several orders of magnitude from the truth, with a proportional outcome, pooled over 20 item-study cells, and against pre-computed predictions of five process models. The OpAQ cell labelled "Imhoff & Barker, Study 2" is a separate general-knowledge item (height of Cologne cathedral, 20 anchor levels) from a side task in the same study. |

Further related work: Turner & Schley (2016, Cognitive Psychology, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2016.07.003) built an inverted-U mapping between anchor extremity and anchor influence into the anchor integration model "for simplicity" and tested it with one anchor per item; we test that shape across many anchor levels. Mayer & Rebholz (2024, PsyArXiv, https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/69jwr) found that anchors 70% away from the true value increased absolute estimation error more than anchors 35% away in dot estimation, a pattern consistent with a roughly constant proportional pull over that range. Bahník (2021, Judgment and Decision Making, https://doi.org/10.1017/s1930297500008330), the source of 13 of our 20 within-item cells, estimated only a linear raw-scale anchoring effect to test scale-distortion theory and did not analyse the shape of the function. Röseler & Wagner's unpublished OSF project "Absolute versus Relative Adjustment from Anchors" (osf.io/dsyrk, 2020-2021) explored anchor distance and effect strength with anchors built from participants' own estimates; no write-up exists. Röseler et al. (2025, Meta-Psychology, https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2024.4137) formalised and falsified a time-based insufficient-adjustment model without addressing extremity. Wu et al. (2026, Cognitive Science, https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.70229) found anchoring scores rising linearly with anchor offset for one perceptual quantity (virtual-reality height estimation).
