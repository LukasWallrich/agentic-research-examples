# The five weakest points of the paper

My own assessment as the AI system that designed and wrote the study, ordered from most to least damaging.

## 1. The primary finding rests on one study with an unusual kind of anchor

Thirteen of the 20 within-item cells and 83% of the within-item estimates come from Bahník (2021), in which participants spun a slot-machine-like device to generate random anchors between 1 and 1,000 for 13 lengths, heights and distances. Without this study, the pooled curvature is uninformative (95% CI roughly −0.36 to 0.27). The other studies do not form a clean replication. The two Yoon et al. items used participants' own height or age as anchors and showed the opposite (positive) curvature. The four Frech et al. items had anchors only above the truth and were imprecise. The one other item with many anchor levels and a clear negative curvature (Imhoff & Barker's Cologne cathedral) is coded as fixed in OpAQ, but its authors describe the anchors as random. The paper can claim that estimates respond less to far anchors for random anchors in these tasks. It has no informative within-item test for the deliberate, informative anchors that matter in negotiation, pricing or law.

## 2. The model comparison is weaker than the preregistration makes it look

The five models were calibrated to a single external benchmark (Jacowitz & Kahneman's index of about .49). That index was computed on the log scale in the preregistered calibration, although the original is a raw-scale value; the raw-scale recalibration reached the same conclusions. The predictive intervals depend on auxiliary assumptions that no data constrain: the spread of beliefs across people, response noise, item uncertainty from 0.3 to 1.2 log units, uniform sampling over grid points. The preregistered survival rule kept only the resource-rational model, and only because its predictions were wide. The model that best reproduces the within-item data, the plausible-boundary model M3, failed on the across-cell slope, which is itself confounded. No model was fitted to the data. The paper therefore rules out two simple implementations (fixed-step adjustment and log-scale constant weighting) and is suggestive about the rest. It does not identify the process.

## 3. Ruling out raw-scale averaging rests on an exploratory analysis

The preregistered pooled test cannot distinguish log-scale plausibility models from simple averaging on the raw scale: both predict a negative pooled curvature. The raw-scale model was not in the preregistration. It was added after the citation check showed that Imhoff & Barker found a linear raw-scale relation for one of the analysed items. What separates the two accounts is the sign of the curvature above the true value. That estimate is exploratory, comes mostly from the same Bahník study, and uses a quadratic term that is sensitive to where anchors happen to fall. The paper states this, but the claim that "plausibility limits anchor influence" depends on it more than the abstract conveys.

## 4. Extremity is measured against the truth, not against what people believe or know

Extremity is the log distance of the anchor from the true value. Anchoring theories concern distance from the person's belief and its uncertainty. Within items this matters less, because the belief distribution is fixed. Across items it may be why the across-cell analysis did not show the within-item decline: researchers may have placed anchors further from the truth for quantities that people know poorly. The exploratory attempts to address this gave conflicting answers: a ratio of extremity to response spread gave a negative slope, and spread as a separate predictor gave a positive one. Both use outcome-contaminated spreads, so they cannot settle it. The across-cell result (inconclusive, positive-leaning, and not robust to clustering or influential cells) remains unexplained.

## 5. The data are harmonised secondary data, and the whole study was produced by AI without human verification

OpAQ codes do not always match the original designs. For two of the four within-item studies, the anchor-type codes do not match how the original authors described their anchors. One across-cell item had responses in different units from its anchors. A corrigendum by the dataset's own team suggests item mix-ups in another study. We followed the preregistered coding, so these issues flow into the confirmatory results. Separately, every step (question choice, model specification, code, interpretation, response to reviews) was done by an AI system and reviewed by other AI systems. The author has not yet checked the work. Model-generated errors that all AI reviewers missed, for example in the simulation assumptions or the reading of cited papers, remain possible until a human expert reviews the code, the model implementations and the literature claims.
