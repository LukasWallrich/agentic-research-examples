| 9 | 2026-09-27 | Commit to the OpAQ anchoring study (option A) over the other shortlisted options (risk-task noise simulation, noise-only overconfidence in GJP quizzes, frozen social-influence model). | Codex and agy independently recommended A. | 10 | 2026-09-27 | Primary test is within-item: in cells with at least three distinct anchor values, test whether the local elasticity of log estimates with respect to log anchors rises or falls with anchor extremity. The across-cell regression of the anchoring index on extremity is a secondary test. | Within an item the belief distribution is fixed, so changes in elasticity across anchors cannot come from item selection. Across cells, anchors were chosen by the original authors together with the items (codex, agy). |
| 11 | 2026-09-27 | Extremity is measured without outcome data: x = log(anchor / true value). No standardisation by the SD of anchored responses. | SDs of anchored responses are themselves affected by anchoring (agy, codex). Dates, temperatures, percentages, preferences, valuations without a true value, and closed or visual scales are excluded because the log ratio is not meaningful or responses are bounded. |
| 12 | 2026-09-27 | Exclude Röseler & Wagner (2021) from all sets. | Their anchors were computed from each participant's unanchored estimates and participants were excluded on anchored responses (their analysis_prereg.R), so anchor values depend on the outcome. |
| 13 | 2026-09-27 | Exclude anchortype 4 (self-generated) and 5 (incidental/subliminal; defined in OpAQ labels.xlsx). Random anchors (type 1) stay in the within-item set but are analysed also as a separate stratum. | Self-generated and incidental anchors have no presented anchor value comparable to the other types. Random anchors are the bulk of the within-item data (Bahník, 2021a); the models make the same shape predictions for them, but their overall effect may be small. |
| 14 | 2026-09-27 | Model predictions come from simulations on the exact OpAQ designs (anchor values, true values, participants, cell sizes; design columns only), with each model calibrated to Jacowitz & Kahneman's (1995) anchoring index (.30-.60 band around .49). The simulated data pass through the same estimator code as the real data. Predictions are frozen in the preregistration. | Codex and the advisor: predictions must be fixed before data contact, and simulated responses must go through the same bounds, transformations, exclusions and estimator. Calibrating to an external finding keeps the OpAQ outcomes out of the parameter choice. |
| 15 | 2026-09-27 | Add a gate: the shape test is interpreted only if the average elasticity b is credibly positive (95% CI lower bound above 0). Report relative curvature rho = g / b for the equivalence decision. | If anchoring is absent (possible for random anchors), g is near zero for a trivial reason and would be misread as support for constant-weight integration (advisor). rho removes the dependence of g on overall anchoring strength. |
| 16 | 2026-09-27 | Gross-error rule: drop responses whose log distance from the truth exceeds both log(1000) and the cell's largest anchor distance plus log(10). | Removes unit errors and typing slips without removing any response that equals an anchor, so the rule cannot bias the test against strong anchoring. |
| 17 | 2026-09-27 | Add a resource-rational variant (M1R) in which the number of adjustments minimises expected absolute error plus a time cost (Lieder et al., 2018a, Eq. 2), next to the fixed-adjustment version (M1). M1R gets a parameter kappa (the agent's own uncertainty relative to the spread of beliefs across people). | The novelty check showed that Lieder et al.'s optimal number of adjustments grows with anchor distance, which can reverse the predicted shape. Without kappa, M1R cannot reproduce Jacowitz & Kahneman's index (minimum .77), because anchors within about one belief SD are never adjusted. Lieder et al. calibrated on elicited individual uncertainty, which can be narrower than the spread across people. |
| 18 | 2026-09-27 | Equivalence bounds: rho in (-0.05, 0.05) for H1; d in (-0.03, 0.03) for H2. The equivalence check is applied before the sign tests. | Bounds sit just below the smallest effect inside the 95% predictive interval of any model with a determinate sign, and well outside M2's interval. Checking equivalence first stops a negligible but significant curvature (likely with 8,000 rows) from being attributed to M1 while the model-adequacy test contradicts M1 (advisor). |
| 19 | 2026-09-27 | Add gate G2 for H2 (mean log anchoring index credibly above 0) and treat the raw-scale index (R4) as descriptive only. | Without G2, data with no anchoring look "flat" in H2 (97.5% of M0 replicates). A dry run with M2 data gave a falling raw-scale slope, so R4 cannot discriminate between log-scale models. |
| 20 | 2026-09-27 | Model survival (H3) is decided by shape statistics (curvature and across-cell slope); level statistics (b, mean index) are reported but do not decide survival. | Levels inherit the calibration to Jacowitz & Kahneman's design and task, which need not transfer to OpAQ; shapes follow from model structure. |
