## Formal-claim attribution

The analytic identities and selection boundary are introduced as direct derivations, with the paired-discordance identity connected to classical correlated-proportion comparisons. The cited literature is not used to validate these identities or assert priority. The Discussion distinguishes a future display-to-estimator hypothesis from observed human behaviour. The opening suggestion that disagreement and high confidence *may* influence scrutiny is framed as a possibility that defines the model; the paper does not claim an established population frequency or causal estimate for it.

The introduction's phrase “the contribution here” identifies the specific analysis rather than making an unrestricted first-ever claim. The restriction to global adoption, correct verification, fixed selection, and known weights is stated. There is no citation-based justification for interpreting the findings as complementary team performance, and the manuscript expressly declines that interpretation.

## Elwin predecessor and restrained novelty

The Introduction explicitly treats naive summaries as candidate rules and cites constructivist coding as evidence that people need not merely repeat the frequencies of observed feedback. The paper identifies the narrower paired-source comparison, where agreements contribute zero, as its target. This respects Elwin et al.'s conceptual precedent and the distinction between their reconstruction of missing outcomes and this model's use of paired correctness. The post-preregistration addendum to `novelty.md` also records their prior use of approximately matched feedback budgets. No broad priority claim about selective feedback preserving performance or the importance of representation remains.
