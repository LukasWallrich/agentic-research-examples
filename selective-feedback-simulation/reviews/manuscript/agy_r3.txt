## 2. Evaluation of Scope, Task Boundaries, and Refutability

### Bounded Global-Adoption Task vs. Casewise Routing
Objections that global adoption is suboptimal compared to casewise routing conflate the chosen decision problem with errors within that problem. The paper does not claim that global adoption is an unconstrained optimal policy; rather, it formalizes a common organizational constraint: delegating an entire future batch to one source when item-level routing is unavailable or cost-prohibitive. The scope is sufficiently and rigorously justified:
- The paper explicitly derives the performance of the confidence-contingent oracle (85.0% crossing, 80.0% homogeneous).
- It explains that within-stratum paired evaluation identifies conditional signs without between-stratum weighting, but notes that sparse strata introduce finite-sample noise.
- The distinction between estimation accuracy and finite-sample decision risk is maintained throughout.

### Refutability of the Future Behavioural Extension
The future experiment avoids the pitfall of treating simulated agents as human empirical findings:
- It specifies exact directional orderings on realised scores rather than asymptotic limits.
- It formulates an explicit two-parameter response family ($\beta, \lambda$) and requires parameters to be shared across schedules to prevent post-hoc accommodation.
- It identifies unambiguous falsification criteria: a reversed empirical ordering rejects the directional account; an interval concentrated near zero rejects a practically relevant display effect.
- It isolates the locus of inference by requiring third-party evaluation with explicit marginal benchmarks first, separating feedback interpretation from self-serving or egocentric bias, which is appropriately reserved for a separate yoked/replay design.

### Minor Issues (Actionable)

1. **Table 3 Subgroup Partition Across Feedback Budgets**  
   In Table 3 (*Registered environment counts comparing weighted with paired regret at selection ratio 8*), the partition into Superior (54), Inferior (24), and Tied (3) environments is presented only for the 500-label budget. The 20-label row displays only the aggregate "All" count ($44 / 22 / 15$). While the text explains that the full machine-readable outputs contain all cells, add a brief parenthetical or table footnote clarifying that the subgroup partition is shown at $N=500$ specifically to illustrate the asymptotic regime where weighting reliably separates superior from inferior advisors.

2. **Explicit Linking of Polynomial Notation to Tie-Breaking in Discrete Convolution**  
   In the subsection *Exact finite-feedback risk*, the formula defines expected regret for $\Delta \ne 0$ as:
   $$R_m(n)=|\Delta|\left\{\sum_{s:\,s\Delta<0}[z^s]G_m(z)^n+\tfrac12[z^0]G_m(z)^n\right\}.$$
   To ensure complete clarity for readers who may not routinely work with generating functions, add a brief clause explicitly stating that the term $[z^0]G_m(z)^n$ extracts the exact probability of a tie ($\tilde S_m = 0$), matching the fair random coin toss defined in the simulation decision rule.

3. **Concrete Finite-Sample Fallback Rules for Post-Stratification**  
   In the *Method* section discussing encounter-based post-stratification ($\widehat\Delta = \sum_c \widehat{p_c d_c}\,\overline{(A-H)}_{V,D,c}$), the text states: *"Empty verified strata require an additional finite-sample rule."* Provide one or two concrete illustrative examples of such rules (e.g., falling back to the unweighted paired mean across verified cases, applying a Laplace/Bayesian prior, or pooling across adjacent confidence bins) so that researchers implementing this estimator have clear guidance on how empty strata are typically handled in practice.

4. **Consistency of Document Title Block / Frontmatter**  
   The provided markdown body begins directly at `# Abstract`. Ensure that in the final compiled outputs (LaTeX/PDF/HTML), the top matter consistently renders the complete document metadata, including the full article title, the designated author byline, and the status note (*"Autonomous draft; author approval pending"*), to preserve traceability across all distributed formats.
