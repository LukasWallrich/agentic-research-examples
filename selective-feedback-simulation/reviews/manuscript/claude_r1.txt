## Answer

# Referee report, round 1: "Learning from disagreements when selective feedback helps and harms advisor evaluation"

## Summary of the submission

The paper separates two tasks: estimating an advisor's absolute accuracy, and deciding whether the advisor is more accurate than oneself. It uses an identity: on binary tasks, agreements add nothing to E[A−H]. From this it argues three things:

- Paired summaries of uniformly sampled disagreements keep the sign of the global advantage Δ.
- Confidence-dependent verification can reverse that sign.
- Inverse-probability weighting (IPW) restores the limiting sign, but it can raise finite-sample regret.

A preregistered Monte Carlo study tests two contrasts (H1 and H2). Both are "supported". The paper proposes a display × selection experiment and states that the behavioural side is untested.

I checked the arithmetic by hand and it is internally consistent:

- **Crossing scenario:**
  - The joint tables give d = 0.30 and Δ = 0.05.
  - E[A−H|D] = 0.167 and E[A|D] = 0.583.
  - At k = 4, the selected yield is 0.15, which gives the contrast −0.167 and 666.7 opportunities per 100 labels.
- **Homogeneous scenario:** d = 0.25, yield 0.075 at k = 8, which gives 266.7 opportunities per 20 labels.
- **Regret:** regret = 5 pp × P(wrong choice). This reproduces Table 1 from Table 2. For example, 5 × (1 − 0.047) ≈ 4.763.
- **Intervals:** z(0.9875) = 2.24, and 2.24 × SE reproduces both intervals.
- **Grid totals:** 44 + 22 + 15 = 81 and 36 + 9 + 36 = 81.

The paper states its limits unusually honestly. The main problems are in what the simulation can add, how the scenarios and benchmark were chosen, and how the behavioural test is identified.

## Major issues

**M1. The simulation confirms outcomes that follow from the set-up.**
- Every learner is a stated function of multinomial counts in four cells: low-confidence advisor-only, low-confidence human-only, high-confidence advisor-only and high-confidence human-only.
- So P(adopt) at n = 20, 100 or 500 can be calculated exactly by enumerating the multinomial (or by a normal approximation). No Monte Carlo is needed.
- The results of H1 and H2 follow closely from facts the paper states before the simulation:
  - In the crossing scenario at k = 4, the limiting paired sign is wrong and the weighted sign is right.
  - In the homogeneous scenario, selection moves mass towards high-confidence disagreements. There the per-disagreement contrast is 0.5, against 0.125 at low confidence. Weighting then puts weight back on the noisy stratum.
- A Bonferroni-adjusted 97.5% interval with SE about 0.01 on a quantity fixed by the set-up gives the look of an empirical test that had a real chance of failing.
- **Fix:**
  - Present the focal results as exact (or enumerated) quantitative consequences, and remove "supported" and "hypothesis" wording.
  - Alternatively, state plainly that the preregistration binds only the reporting choices. It does not bind any uncertain outcome.
  - Move the Monte Carlo to a check against the exact values. The paper already does this for the uniform conditions.
  - If the H1/H2 framing is kept, say in the abstract that the "confirmation" is a check that the code implements the model.

**M2. The focal scenarios sit on knife-edges, and the control is confounded.**
- **Knife-edges in the crossing scenario.** The selected paired sign is zero when k = pδ_L / ((1−p)|δ_H|) = 0.5·0.2 / (0.5·0.1) = 2. At k = 2 the limiting paired contrast is exactly 0. Also, the capped learner (cap 2) at k = 4 targets 0.5·0.2·(1/4)·2 − 0.5·0.1 = 0 exactly. This is why its adoption rates are 49.2% and 49.9% in Table 2. Its "compromise" performance in the focal scenario is therefore a tie artefact of the chosen numbers. Discussion statements about capping ("an interpretable compromise") are uninformative in this scenario.
- **Confounded control.** The homogeneous control keeps the marginal accuracies the same. It changes two further things:
  - the disagreement rate (0.25 vs 0.30);
  - how informative each stratum's disagreements are.

  So H1 vs H2 does not isolate "sign crossing vs no crossing". The comparison also changes signal-to-noise and selection efficiency. In addition, H1 and H2 use different k and n (4/100 vs 8/20), so the two contrasts are not parallel.
- **Fix:**
  - Report the reversal threshold k* = pδ_L / ((1−p)|δ_H|) and the cap-neutral point in closed form.
  - Flag the k = 2 and cap = 2 ties explicitly.
  - Report both focal environments at matched (k, n), using the grid values that already exist.
  - Describe the control as differing in more than the sign structure.
  - Say whether the scenario values were chosen with the analytic thresholds in view.

**M3. The loss benchmark forces the weighting question.**
- Regret is measured against the better fixed source. In the crossing scenario, the natural policy that confidence-stratified feedback supports is to delegate to the advisor at low confidence and keep one's own judgement at high confidence. That gives 0.5·0.80 + 0.5·0.90 = 0.85. Both "correct" global choices give 0.80.
- A learner that pairs within strata needs no weights at all. Per-stratum paired signs are unaffected by between-stratum selection.
- So IPW is needed only because the task forbids the use of the information that makes weighting necessary. The paper says so in one sentence. The benchmark still frames the headline result, so this is more than a scope note.
- **Fix:**
  - Report the stratum-contingent oracle accuracy and the gap it opens, as an analytic result that needs no new simulation.
  - Justify the global-adoption restriction with real cases, for example licensing or procurement decisions where per-case routing is not possible.
  - Add to the Discussion that stratified pairing dominates weighting when confidence is recorded.
  - Temper "correcting selection can help or harm" to "within a global-adoption task".

**M4. The weighted estimand and its information requirements are understated.**
- **What the weighted learner needs:**
  - the correct selection ratio k;
  - the confidence stratum of every verified case;
  - calibrated strata, which hold here by construction.

  With stratum labels, a within-stratum analysis is available (see M3). Without them, IPW is impossible.
- **Estimand statements to fix:**
  - The self-normalised (Hájek) estimator targets E[A−H|D] = Δ/d. The paper states this correctly.
  - The formula p·δ_L + (1−p)·k·δ_H needs δ_c defined as E[A−H | stratum c]. This definition must not condition on disagreement, and the text leaves it ambiguous.
  - Under random all-case feedback, the absolute learner is given the true h. Its variance is then lower than the paired learner's (88.9% vs 81.9% adoption). This benchmark advantage should be stated where Table 2 is discussed.
  - The capped estimator's target should be written out explicitly.
- **Missing sensitivity result:** the paper concedes that misspecified k "may substantially change" performance, but gives no result. The limiting sign under assumed ratio k̂ ≠ k follows in closed form.
- **Fix:** give that closed-form limiting sign as an analytic result, and define δ_c explicitly.

**M5. Resource matching favours selective schedules.**
- Holding labels fixed while the selective schedules use 2–13 times more opportunities is one defensible axis. But random all-case feedback spends about 70% of its labels on agreements, which the paired learners cannot use. So "random" is a weak comparison condition under label matching.
- The opportunity costs are reported but never used in any comparison, and the grid conclusions are drawn only at matched labels.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a plot of regret against expected opportunities, recomputed from the saved outputs or by enumeration. Or state the break-even exchange rate at which the ranking of policies flips.
  - Compare against a uniform-disagreement schedule at matched opportunities. That is the relevant counterfactual for the claim that "selection concentrates informative comparisons".

**M6. The behavioural experiment is not identified as described.**
- **Mechanical predictions.** If participants see a paired display that is positive and an advisor-only display at 58% against a benchmark of 75%, the predicted interaction follows from people following the displayed number. It does not reveal inference from selective feedback. The design cannot tell display compliance from the proposed "display-to-estimator mapping".
- **Selection must be communicated.** Histories are fixed and participants do not produce the confidence judgements. So participants must be told how cases were selected, for "confidence selection" to exist for them. Telling them the rule is itself an intervention: it makes selection-aware inference possible and cues it.
- **The adjusted display is the answer.** A "selection-adjusted display" hands participants the answer, so "restoring adoption" tests trust in the display.
- **No quantitative prediction.** No choice model is given (for example a logistic or softmax mapping from score to choice), and there is no smallest effect of interest. The claim that the account is "vulnerable to clear outcomes" therefore has no threshold.
- **Fix:**
  - Include conditions where the display and the raw history diverge, for example raw trial-by-trial feedback with no summary.
  - Say whether the selection rule is disclosed, crossing disclosure as a factor if needed.
  - State a response model and qualitative orderings over at least three cells, including the inferior-advisor control, which differ between these candidate accounts:
    - naive absolute learning;
    - naive paired learning;
    - selection-aware learning;
    - display compliance.
  - Name the result pattern that would reject each account.

**M7. The reference support is not verifiable and important JDM work is missing.**
- In the supplied text the reference list is empty. I cannot check the entries, and this review was not authorised to use external tools.
- Denrell (2005), Denrell and March (2001) and Fetchenhauer and Dunning (2010) match work I know. The attributions to Pescetelli (2021), Jaquiery (2024), Fok (2024), Honeycutt (2020) and Gao (2021) need checking against the sources.
- Zavattari (2026) and Li (2026) are preprints that I cannot assess. The priority claim leans on them.
- **Missing and directly relevant literature:**
  - selective and constructivist feedback: Einhorn and Hogarth (1978); Elwin, Juslin, Olsson and Enkvist (2007); Le Mens and Denrell (2011); Fiedler on metacognitive myopia;
  - advisor reputation and weighting: Yaniv and Kleinberger (2000);
  - reactions to observed algorithm errors: Dietvorst et al. (2015);
  - the statistical "selective labels" problem: Lakkaraju et al. (2017);
  - the standard sources for the discordant-pair logic (McNemar) and the Horvitz–Thompson and Hájek estimators.

  Elwin et al. in particular already study learning from selective feedback in JDM, so the novelty statement must engage with that work.
- **Fix:**
  - Render the full reference list.
  - Verify each claim against its source, with DOIs checked through Crossref.
  - Add the missing literature above. I name these from memory, so the metadata must be verified.
  - Restate the novelty claim against that literature.

**M8. The AI disclosure and authorship statement contradict each other.**
- The paper lists one human author, yet states that "no claim of completed human oversight is made" and that the whole study was carried out by AI agents on a single day. Authorship under COPE and ICMJE norms requires the author to accept accountability. The manuscript cannot be submitted in this state.
- **Details to fix in the disclosure:**
  - Model identifiers are incomplete. The "Claude CLI default" has no version.
  - "coarse-ink 1.4.0" is not identified.
  - Which steps a human verified is not stated.
- **The preregistration needs an external timestamp.** It is a local git commit, which can be rewritten. The claim that an earlier project's outputs in repository history were "not inspected" can only be taken on trust.
- **Fix:**
  - Deposit the preregistration and code with an independent timestamp, for example OSF or a pushed tag with its hash cited.
  - Record which analyses, derivations and citations the human author has independently checked.
  - Complete the model versions or state why they are unavailable.
  - Remove the contradiction by completing and declaring human review before submission.

## Minor issues

- **m1. Duplicated results.** The first Discussion paragraph repeats the Results word for word. Replace it with interpretation. Also fix "simulation show".
- **m2. Ambiguous abstract phrase.** "Can favour the inferior source" is ambiguous. With h > 0.5 throughout the grid, the absolute learner under uniform disagreement feedback errs only by rejecting a superior advisor. Say which direction.
- **m3. Tolerance units.** The regret tolerance of 0.0005 is in proportion units (0.05 pp), but all other regrets are in pp. State the units.
- **m4. Tie probability.** Ties within 1e-12 have non-trivial probability at n = 20 for the paired score. Report the tie probability per condition, because the random tie-break contributes directly to regret.
- **m5. Undefined assumptions.** "Truth can be taken as balanced, with symmetric decisions" is too vague. State that under binary responses, disagreement is equivalent to exactly one source being correct, and that this is all the model uses.
- **m6. Dependence parameter r.** At r = 1 or 0, some disagreement cells are empty. For example, at r = 1 in a stratum where a > h, H-only = 0, so the paired contrast has no variance. Note how this drives the grid counts.
- **m7. Grid reporting.** Report the 44/22/15 and 36/9/36 splits separately for superior-advisor, inferior-advisor and tie environments. The tie environments are within tolerance trivially.
- **m8. Defensive and rebuttal phrasing.** Several sentences argue against claims no reader has made. Examples: "not a new statistical identity", "It is not a general claim that…", "not a claim that people know their accuracy", "not optimised to the observed results", "not independently timestamped". State the positive facts once, in Method.
- **m9. Figure 1.** Add Monte Carlo (or exact) error marks. Consider a log-scaled budget axis. Label the panels with k.
- **m10. Title.** It lacks punctuation ("…disagreements: when…").
- **m11. Code availability.** "Local research package" is not an availability statement. Give an archive plan with a persistent identifier.

## Verdict

**Major issues remain.** The algebra, the numbers and the stated limits are sound and internally consistent. But four problems stop the paper in its current form:

- The "confirmatory" results are simulated confirmations of consequences programmed into the model (M1).
- The focal and capped comparisons rest on knife-edge parameter values, and the control is confounded (M2).
- The benchmark suppresses the obvious stratified alternative (M3).
- The behavioural test cannot separate inference from display compliance (M6).

In addition, the disclosure and authorship statement is not yet compatible with submission (M8). Most fixes are analytic or presentational and need no new human data. The paper is honest about its behavioural claims being untested, so the absence of human data is not in itself a reason to reject. Revise and resubmit for round 2.
