## Assessment of the first-round major concerns

1. **Deployment benchmark: resolved by defensible scope restriction.** The manuscript defines global adoption at the outset and gives constrained batch handoff as its application. It reports the confidence-contingent oracle and explicitly disclaims superiority over a learned routing policy. Global regret is valid for this restricted action space. A reader should not infer that it measures all achievable human–advisor complementarity. Requiring a new routing analysis would change the decision problem rather than repair an error within it.

2. **Advantage-pattern coverage: substantially addressed, with limited external reach.** E3 contains 36 environments and 2,160 exact learner-condition evaluations, including all four conditional advantage-sign patterns at common selection ratios and budgets. Its six marginal-tie environments are retained. The reported ranges match `output/exploratory_grid.json`: at ratio 4 and 100 labels, the two crossing patterns span both benefits and costs of weighting; the same-sign patterns span equality and costs. The computation retains overlap and mixture variation, and the manuscript states that the advantage magnitude remains narrowly fixed. This answers the absence of inferior-both and opposite-crossing comparisons. It does not justify a prevalence estimate or a claim that the sign pattern alone determines finite-sample risk. No such claim is now made.

3. **Known selection and positivity: adequately bounded rather than empirically resolved.** The analytic misspecification threshold and E2 show that the finite-label and limiting results depend on the assumed weight. The Discussion now states both within-stratum ignorability and positive-support requirements. It explicitly says that the positive-weight sensitivity does not identify an unverified stratum or repair outcome-related selection within strata. This is an acceptable limitation for a benchmark scenario study. It would remain a major gap if the paper recommended confidence-only adjustment for arbitrary voluntary verification; the current version does not do so. Minor comment R2-1 below improves placement of this condition.

4. **Equal opportunity comparison: resolved for the stated focal comparisons.** E1 includes unaudited opportunities as zero score contributions and gives exact finite-encounter risks. Its random-all-case versus uniform-disagreement paired-risk identity follows from agreements contributing zero. The focal directions persist at fixed encounters, while magnitudes and expected label counts change. The manuscript does not claim an optimal acquisition policy under unmodelled waiting-time or opportunity costs.

5. **Behavioural evidence versus computational development: the computational route is now explicit.** The Laurent-polynomial formula gives the finite-label score distribution and its induced regret, with fair ties and marginal ties handled correctly. This is a computable risk boundary, not a learner-accessible optimal rule or a new empirical finding. The behavioural section now distinguishes supplied displays from unaided computation and keeps the cognitive mapping untested. During this follow-up I flagged that limiting absolute-score predictions need not hold for short histories. The current manuscript corrects that issue: it identifies the orderings as large-history limits and requires a future protocol to calculate finite-history predictions before outcomes. No human experiment is required to complete this bounded computational paper.

6. **Grid magnitudes, correction guidance, and caps: sufficiently addressed for a scenario study.** Matched focal exact risks, E3 effect ranges, complete machine-readable outputs, the finite-risk formula, and the analytic capped target provide more than counts of favourable cells. The focal cap-neutral point is identified as a boundary rather than evidence for a useful compromise. The paper still offers no universal rule for choosing weights without knowing the environment; that is an actual limit, stated in the text. A general optimal policy theorem would be a different contribution.

The three detailed first-round comments are addressed: agreements are named as the zero-contribution cases; histories are held fixed across displays within a selection schedule, not across schedules; and the regret categories use explicit inclusive tolerance boundaries.

It prevents the displayed identity from being extracted and applied under a weaker assumption than the model uses.

## Residual limitations that do not require another analysis round

The scientific contribution is modest and conditional: established paired-comparison and selection-weighting ideas are combined in a specific advisor-evaluation task. The paper cannot establish that its scenarios resemble deployment environments, that its contrast magnitudes occur in people, or that people can use the proposed adjustments. E3 broadens sign-pattern coverage while retaining fixed human accuracies and a single absolute advantage magnitude. E2 examines misspecified fixed weights, not estimation error or nonignorable missingness. The finite-risk expression is a transparent numerical decision criterion given the world probabilities, not a closed-form universal recommendation. These are substantive limits to the paper's reach, not problems repaired merely by using the word “exploratory”. The revised claims stay within them.
