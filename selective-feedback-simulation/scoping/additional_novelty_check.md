# Additional novelty check: confidence, agreement, and adaptive ensembles

28 September 2026. This check inspected the primary proceedings paper and its publisher metadata after the manuscript had been drafted. It involved no study outcomes or new simulations.

**Reference:** Amin, H., Yin, M., & Khanna, R. (2026). Align When They Want, Complement When They Need! Human-Centered Ensembles for Adaptive Human-AI Collaboration. *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 40*(21), 17337–17346. DOI: [10.1609/aaai.v40i21.38786](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i21.38786). The primary [publisher page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38786) and a direct Crossref works query agree on the authors Hasan Amin, Ming Yin, and Rajiv Khanna, title, date 14 March 2026, volume, issue, and pages.

**Relevant content:** The [primary PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/38786/42748), “Modeling Human Behavior”, pp. 17339–17340, defines confidence-gated probabilistic reliance. High-confidence human–AI agreement determines reliance in the low-confidence region. The paper analyses a trade-off between alignment and complementary performance and trains specialist models selected through adaptive routing.

**Overlap:** Both accounts distinguish confidence regions and connect disagreement to reliance. Therefore, neither confidence-dependent reliance nor a contrast between alignment and usefulness is an available broad novelty claim for this manuscript.

**Difference:** Amin et al. optimise team behaviour and AI design through confidence-gated reliance; the present paper studies which accuracy comparison follows from a fixed selected history of verified ground-truth labels. The current model's absolute, paired, and weighted feedback summaries are not the same as their alignment-based reliance rule. Their paper does not provide the present comparison of global source-adoption errors under selective labelled feedback.

**Decision:** Retain as an additional scoping source. It is substantively adjacent but does not supersede Pescetelli, Jaquiery, Fetchenhauer, Gao, or Zavattari as one of the five closest papers for the present restricted contribution. A manuscript citation would be appropriate if the introduction were broadened to adaptive human–AI ensembles or confidence-gated reliance; it is not required for a current factual claim.

**Zavattari wording check:** The current `novelty.md` contrasts *learning comparative competence* with *allocating inspections to reduce remaining errors*. That comparison is defensible despite the preprint's audit-driven posterior updates. The manuscript makes the updates explicit. Avoid shortening the distinction to “we study learning whereas they do not”.
