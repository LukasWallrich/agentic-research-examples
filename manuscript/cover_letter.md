Dear Editors of *Judgment and Decision Making*,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Extreme anchors lose proportional pull: A preregistered, model-based test of how anchoring scales with anchor extremity in open trial-level data", as an Empirical Article.

**What the paper does.** Anchoring theories disagree about how an anchor's influence changes as the anchor becomes more extreme, and the two recent meta-analyses reach different conclusions on this question. Much of the disagreement comes from measuring anchor influence as a standardised mean difference, which grows with anchor distance even when anchors pull estimates by a constant proportion. We derived the shape of the anchor-response function from five process models, including two versions of Lieder et al.'s (2018) resource-rational account. We then simulated these models on the exact designs of the Open Anchoring Quest, an openly licensed collection of trial-level anchoring data, and tested the predictions in a preregistered secondary analysis.

**Main findings.** Within items, the proportional pull of an anchor fell as the anchor moved away from the true value. This contradicts anchor-and-adjust with a fixed number of adjustments and constant-weight integration. Across two-anchor studies, where authors chose the anchor values, the anchoring index showed no detectable decline, and no model reproduced both patterns with one parameter set. We report the limits openly: the within-item evidence comes mostly from one study with random anchors, and the across-cell result is sensitive to influential cells.

**Fit with the journal.** The journal's aims name the analysis of existing data and process models, and the journal published one of the studies we analyse (Bahník, 2021). The paper should interest readers who work on anchoring, on process models of judgement, and on how moderator analyses in meta-analyses are read.

**Transparency.** The preregistration (following van den Akker et al.'s secondary-data template) was committed to version control before any outcome variable was read, together with frozen model predictions and the analysis code. All deviations are listed in the manuscript and in a separate file. Code, outputs and the preregistration are openly available, and one script reproduces every number from the openly licensed raw data (CC-BY 4.0). The study used only anonymised secondary data. No new data were collected, and no ethics approval was sought; we can provide further information if the editors require it.
