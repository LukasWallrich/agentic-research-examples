# Journal and open-data scoping

Checked 28 September 2026. Recommended target: *Judgment and Decision Making* (JDM), as a Theory Article if the project uses simulation, or an Empirical Article if it analyses existing data. Its remit covers normative and descriptive judgement, forecasting, process models, and theory development. A model needs to answer a psychological question intelligibly to JDM readers; a generic machine-learning benchmark would have a weaker fit. The journal explicitly permits theoretical work and secondary analysis. [Official scope](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/information/author-instructions).

## Requirements for the intended manuscript

The current preparation page lists Theory Articles. Prepare one manuscript containing a title page, abstract of at most 250 words, at most 10 keywords, main text with embedded figures and editable tables, acknowledgements, competing interests, data availability, financial support, supplementary materials, and references. Supply PDF alongside Word or LaTeX. Use APA author–date citations; include figure captions and accessible descriptions.

AI tools cannot be authors. Declare generated prose in acknowledgements and AI involvement in analysis in Methods. Report tools, versions, dates, access routes, and uses; identify relevant third-party material. Authors remain accountable. This workflow therefore needs an unusually explicit disclosure covering ideation, coding, analysis, writing, and reviewing. Do not state that a human has verified or approved the paper until that has occurred. [Preparation and AI policy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials).

Provide sufficient data, code, and materials to reproduce the work and a data availability statement. A local folder can support this draft; any future submission requires reviewer access, through supplementary files or a suitable repository. The present task authorises neither submission nor public upload. [Transparency policy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/information/journal-policies/research-transparency).

The linked statistical policy asks authors to address sampling uncertainty, distinguish absence of evidence from no effect, test interactions directly, avoid unnecessary categorisation, and consider item as well as participant variation. In a simulation, distinguish Monte Carlo uncertainty from uncertainty about human behaviour: many simulated agents do not establish empirical validity. [Statistical policy](https://jbaron.org/journal/stat.htm).

JDM remains the preferred target. A possible reserve is *Computational Brain & Behavior*: the sponsoring Society for Mathematical Psychology describes a remit including mathematical models, computer simulation, and empirical studies. Its current author and AI policies would need a separate check if selected. [Society journal description](https://www.mathpsych.org/page/cbb).

## Recommendation and decision boundaries

A preregistered simulation is feasible without accessing participant records. It offers direct control over dependence between human and algorithmic errors, confidence calibration, and feedback availability, while avoiding uncertain dataset schemas. It must earn its contribution through a mechanism or boundary condition beyond familiar optimal weighting, with an explicit empirical test that could reject it. The publicly documented datasets above are potential future tests, not validation evidence for an untested simulation.

If a secondary analysis is preferred, the chess dataset has the clearest existing documentation and licence. Before committing to it, determine a question distinct from its original confidence-dynamics model and write a secondary-data preregistration disclosing all documentation seen. For any selected dataset, perform a scripted privacy/schema check before displaying records and stop using files containing direct personal identifiers. No records were acquired in this scoping task.
