### Part 1: Assessment of Earlier Major Issues and Rejected Comments

#### 1. Resolution of Earlier MAJOR Issues

- **Dominance of Bahník (2021) and Item Domain Scope (Comments A1, X4, K1):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  The manuscript features a neutral title; the Abstract explicitly states that 83% of within-item estimates come from a single study using random anchors for lengths, heights, and distances; the Discussion and Conclusion restrict the primary within-item claim to random anchors in physical estimation; and single-study fits (S2) and leave-one-study-out sensitivity checks are transparently reported.

- **Extrapolation to Strategic Applied Settings (Comment A2):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  All prescriptive advice directed at negotiators, litigants, or survey designers claiming that extreme anchors yield diminishing returns has been removed. Applied contexts are retained strictly as motivating questions in the Introduction, and the Discussion and Conclusion explicitly note that whether deliberately chosen, informative anchors exhibit diminishing returns remains an open empirical question.

- **Framing of M1 and Survival of M1R (Comments A3, X1, K2):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  M1 is correctly framed as the mechanistic core of adjustment with a fixed number of steps rather than an independently defended theory. M1R's formal survival under the preregistered rule is explicitly documented as an artifact of an uninformative, overly wide predictive interval for relative curvature $\rho$. The authors added a post-hoc joint check ($b$ and $\gamma$ jointly; analysis R1a), reporting in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion that 0% of M1R replicates reproduced both statistics simultaneously (compared to 33% for M3 and 4% for M4), and explicitly concluding that M1R is "not supported by the within-item data."

- **Selective Reporting of Analysis E4 (Comment A4):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  Both the ratio model ($d = -0.023$, 95% CI $[-0.037, -0.010]$) and the separate-predictor model ($d = 0.028$, $[-0.050, 0.106]$) are reported in the Results and Discussion. The authors discuss the risk of ratio-predictor artifacts and withdrew the claim that E4 resolved the across-cell divergence.

- **Descriptive Inventory of Within-Item Cells (Comment A5):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  Table 1 provides a comprehensive inventory of all 20 cells, specifying study, item, measurement unit, true value, sample size, number of anchor levels, range of $x$, and anchor type.

- **Conflation of Local Elasticity with "Share of Gap" (Comment X2-1):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  The manuscript carefully distinguishes local elasticity ($b + 2\gamma|x|$) from the share of distance between belief and anchor. The definition in the Measures section explicitly states this distinction, and the findings throughout the Abstract, Results, Discussion, and Conclusion describe the empirical pattern as a declining local elasticity or weaker response of estimates to changes in the anchor at extreme distances.

- **Overstatement of Support for Plausibility Models (Comment X2-2):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  The language has been calibrated: the within-item shape is described as "consistent with" plausibility-limited accounts rather than "supporting" them; the Discussion explicitly designates plausibility-limited influence as "one possible account rather than an established mechanism"; and the text prominently acknowledges that no calibrated model explained the combined within-item and across-cell patterns.

- **Within-Item vs. Across-Cell Divergence (Comment K3):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  The Discussion directly addresses why the two designs diverge (confounding of extremity with item uncertainty/difficulty in researcher-chosen designs vs. differences between random and informative anchors), discusses exploratory analyses bearing on the divergence (E4, E7, E10), and proposes an experimental design capable of dissociating the mechanisms.

- **Functional Form and Quadratic Imposition (Comment K4):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  The authors added exploratory analysis E9 (a five-band piecewise linear spline), showing that the local slope falls from 0.33 near the true value to 0.12 at 1–2 log units away and does not turn negative at extreme distances, confirming that the decline is not an artifact of the quadratic parameterization.

- **Precision Fallback and Item Heterogeneity (Comment K6):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  The authors added exploratory analysis E8 (cell-by-cell quadratic fits pooled via random-effects meta-analysis), showing $\gamma = -0.046$ ($95\%\text{ CI } [-0.068, -0.024]$, $\tau = 0.035$, $I^2 = 65\%$, with 16 of 20 cells negative), demonstrating that the fallback model's pooled estimate is robust to item-level curvature heterogeneity.

- **Mathematical Derivation of Hedges' $g$ Growth (Comment K8):**  
  *Status: ADEQUATELY RESOLVED.*  
  Lines 94–99 of the Introduction provide a clean derivation showing that the standardized difference $\beta(x_H - x_L)/s$ grows linearly with anchor distance even when proportional influence $\beta$ and standard deviation $s$ remain constant.

#### 2. Evaluation of Rejected Comments

- **Round 1, Comment A1 (Partial Rejection):**  
  *Reviewer Proposal:* State that 100% of the cells showing significant within-item curvature used random anchors.  
  *Author Response:* Rejected in part, because Imhoff & Barker's Cologne cathedral item (coded as fixed in OpAQ, with 20 anchor levels) also showed significant negative curvature ($\gamma = -0.089$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.148, -0.031]$).  
  *Evaluation:* **Rejected for a sound reason.** The author was factually and statistically correct; reporting the single fixed-anchor multi-level item accurately reflects the empirical record.

- **Round 2, Comment K5 (Rejection):**  
  *Reviewer Proposal:* Add a second empirical calibration target, such as the elasticity observed in a published random-anchor study.  
  *Author Response:* Rejected because any multi-anchor dataset in OpAQ—principally Bahník (2021)—forms part of the confirmatory test data. Calibrating models on the test data would introduce circularity and data leakage.  
  *Evaluation:* **Rejected for an excellent reason.** Preserving the separation between calibration targets and test data is fundamental to preregistered confirmatory model evaluation.

- **Round 2, Comment K2 (Partial Rejection):**  
  *Reviewer Proposal:* Replace the preregistered binary survival rule with the joint reproduction share as the primary confirmatory criterion.  
  *Author Response:* Accepted in part: the joint check was reported prominently in the Abstract and Results, but kept labelled as post-hoc rather than relabelled as primary.  
  *Evaluation:* **Rejected for a sound reason.** Reclassifying a post-hoc analysis as the "preregistered primary test" would undermine open-science integrity.

- **Round 2, Comments K-3 & K-4 (Partial Rejection):**  
  *Reviewer Proposal:* Require external registry timestamp and a statement that the human author had reviewed all analyses.  
  *Author Response:* Accepted in part: the authors disclosed that the timestamp was a local git commit and explained what was inspected prior to the commit; they declined to assert that the human author had already reviewed all analyses because that had not yet occurred.  
  *Evaluation:* **Rejected for sound reasons.** The transparency regarding project constraints and verification status is candid and accurate.

### 1. Numbered List of Remaining Issues

#### [MINOR] 1. Data Inconsistency in Yoon et al. Anchor Range
- **Location:** `scratch_manuscript.md` line 298 (Method: Data); `manuscript.qmd` line 143.
- **Problem:** The text states: *"The entered values range from 8 to 1,500 and from 6 to 279, which suggests entries in other units and some errors..."*  
  However, in Table 1 (line 352) and the underlying data (`output/within_cells_table.csv`, row 20), the anchor values for the Stephen King item range from **6 to 267** ($\ln(267 / 51) = 1.655 \approx 1.7$; $\ln(6 / 51) = -2.140 \approx -2.1$). The figure 279 appears to have been copied from an approximate back-calculation in a Round-2 review comment ($51 \times \exp(1.7) \approx 279.2$) rather than verified against the true empirical maximum of 267.
- **Fix:** Change *"from 6 to 279"* to *"from 6 to 267"*.

#### [MINOR] 2. Contradictory H3 Decision Rule Description in Method vs. Results
- **Location:** `scratch_manuscript.md` lines 482–484 (Method: Analysis plan, H3) vs. lines 628–630 (Results: Model adequacy) and lines 653–654 (Table 3 caption).
- **Problem:** Method line 482 states: *"A model was contradicted when the observed curvature ($\gamma$ or $\rho$) or the across-cell slope $d$ fell outside its 95% predictive interval..."*  
  Taken literally, this implies that a model is contradicted if *either* $\gamma$ or $\rho$ misses. Under that reading, M1R would be contradicted because observed $\gamma$ fell outside M1R's interval ($[-0.029, 0.082]$).  
  In contrast, the committed analysis code (`R/04_confirmatory.R` line 129: `survives_shape := (g_inside | rho_inside) & slope_inside`), the Results text (lines 628–630), and the Table 3 caption define the rule as retaining a model if $d$ falls inside *and at least one* of $\gamma$ or $\rho$ does (meaning contradiction requires *both* $\gamma$ and $\rho$ to fall outside, or $d$ to fall outside). While the Results and Table 3 caption were updated in Round 2, the text in Method line 482 was overlooked and remains inconsistent.
- **Fix:** Revise lines 482–484 in the Method to align with the operational rule:  
  *"A model was contradicted when the across-cell slope $d$ fell outside its 95% predictive interval, or when both curvature statistics ($\gamma$ and $\rho$) fell outside (i.e., a model was retained if $d$ and at least one of $\gamma$ and $\rho$ lay inside its predictive intervals); M0 was also contradicted if gate G1 passed."*

#### [MINOR] 3. Statistical Qualification of Multi-Band Piecewise Estimates at Extreme Distances
- **Location:** `scratch_manuscript.md` lines 790–792 (Results: Exploratory analyses, *Shape of the response function*).
- **Problem:** The text states: *"The slope thus fell over the first two log units and then stayed low but positive, without the reversal that the quadratic term implies beyond $|x| \approx 3.78$."*  
  While the point estimates remain positive ($0.12$ for $|x| \in [2, 3)$ and $0.06$ for $|x| \geq 3$), the 95% confidence intervals at these extreme distances span zero ($[-0.02, 0.27]$ and $[-0.05, 0.18]$, per `output/exploratory/E9_band_slopes.csv`) due to sparser observations ($n = 561$ and $n = 147$).
- **Fix:** Qualify the statement slightly to clarify that point estimates remained positive, though confidence intervals included zero at extreme distances (e.g., *"stayed low and positive in point estimate, though spanning zero beyond $|x| = 2$ due to sparser observations"*).

#### [MINOR] 4. Internal Table Numbering / Appendix Labeling
- **Location:** `scratch_manuscript.md` line 400 and line 1304; `review_log.md` line 133.
- **Problem:** Line 133 of `review_log.md` states: *"Accepted. Appendix Table A1 lists every distributional assumption."* In the manuscript, however, the table is cross-referenced as "Table 4 in the Appendix" (line 400) and titled "Table 4: Assumptions of the model simulations..." (line 1304). While consecutive numbering (Tables 1–4) is mathematically orderly, standard APA guidelines typically designate appendix tables as Table A1.
- **Fix:** Ensure consistent cross-referencing across the manuscript and production files (e.g., maintain Table 4 or rename to Table A1 throughout).

#### [MINOR] 5. Public Repository Placeholder Link

- **Fix:** As already noted in `deviations.md` and `ai_disclosure.md`, this is an expected placeholder due to sandbox/account constraints.
