## Major issues (still open)

**1. The claim that weighting "requires the correct sampling ratio" is too strong. A feasible estimator that needs no known ratio is not discussed.**
The initial responses are observed before any verification, so a decision maker can see a disagreement without learning the truth. They can therefore count encountered disagreements per confidence stratum (to estimate d_c and p) as well as verified disagreements. From those counts they can estimate the verification ratio, or post-stratify directly: Δ̂ = Σ p̂_c d̂_c × (paired mean in stratum c). This recovers the sign of Δ, and its magnitude, without being told k. Its only requirement is conditional ignorability, meaning selection within a stratum does not depend on which source is right.

This affects four parts of the paper:
- (a) In the Methods, "weighting additionally requires … the correct sampling ratio", and in the Scope section, "known selection weights … favour the statistical corrections". Both need qualifying.
- (b) The misspecified-weight analysis tests a failure that the available information could partly prevent.
- (c) In the Analytic framework, the statement that a non-zero adoption cost requires d and hence the magnitude of Δ is correct, but d can be observed without labels.
- (d) The "selection-aware" behavioural account could use the observed disagreement counts, not a disclosed schedule.

The fix is textual: say that k and d_c can be observed from encounters, and say whether the finite-sample costs are expected to carry over to the estimated-weight or post-stratified version. An exploratory exact check at the focal cells would be better still, and cheap with the existing convolution code. This does not threaten the registered contrasts. It does limit how widely the "correction can harm" message applies.

**2. The registered grid contains only one crossing direction, and the manuscript does not say so.**
The human accuracies are fixed at 0.60 (low confidence) and 0.90 (high confidence). The advisor's low-confidence accuracies are all at least 0.65, and its high-confidence accuracies are all at most 0.90. So every grid environment has δ_L > 0 ≥ δ_H. High-confidence oversampling therefore always pushes towards the human. It can reverse the ordering only for superior advisors, and it always reinforces the correct ordering for inferior advisors.

This structure explains the grid counts: 0 improvements from weighting among inferior advisors, and all 24 limiting reversals among superior advisors. Without it stated, the 44/22/15 and 36/9/36 counts read like breadth. The sign-pattern extension partly fills the gap, but only at ±0.05 advantages. Requested changes:
- State this structure where the grid is described.
- Interpret the grid counts as conditional on the δ_L > 0 ≥ δ_H pattern.
- Point readers to the sign-pattern extension as the only evidence on the other patterns, with its narrow magnitude restated.

**3. The proposed behavioural test changes the self-versus-advisor question, and one account's prediction is under-specified.**
The test's refutability is set out honestly: histories are fixed, disclosure is manipulated, parameters are shared across schedules, and thresholds are left to a future protocol. Two problems remain.
- (a) The paper's question is about comparing *one's own* judgements with an advisor's. In the proposed design, histories are generated in advance and held fixed across display conditions. The "human" responses a participant sees are then not their own, which turns the task into judging between two described sources. Self–other asymmetry, which the Discussion lists as a rival explanation, is then ruled out by design, not tested. The protocol should choose one of two routes: yoke or replay each participant's own earlier judgements (with any consequences for holding histories fixed), or state openly that the test concerns evaluating two third-party sources.
- (b) The absolute account predicts below-chance adoption only relative to a marginal human benchmark. That benchmark is supplied to the simulated learner, but the proposal never says how participants would get it (shown, elicited, or learned). Without that step, the absolute and paired predictions cannot be told apart in the raw-history condition. The prediction also holds only when Δ < d(2h − 1). That condition is met in the crossing task and should be stated next to the prediction.

## Minor issues

4. **Abstract.** It sets the two contrasts side by side as if the sign pattern were the only difference ("the same marginal source accuracies"). In fact H1 and H2 also differ in ratio (4 vs 8) and budget (100 vs 20), and "at the smaller budget" does not make this clear. Add one clause citing the matched result, which keeps the same direction at matched settings (Table 3).
5. **Positivity and ignorability.** The Analytic framework says inverse weights recover the sign "provided both disagreement strata have positive sampling probability". Conditional ignorability should be stated there as well. At present it appears only in Scope.
6. **Identical minima in the sign-pattern extension.** Both crossing patterns show the same minimum, −2.310, although the joint distributions are not mirror images: the human accuracies differ and the q formula is not symmetric under flipping the sign of δ. Please check that this is a genuine coincidence, for example the same boundary environment, and not a reporting error.
7. **Review history in the manuscript.** The heading "Exploratory checks requested in review" should be replaced by "Exploratory analyses" with a note that they were added after the registered results. Several phrases read as replies to earlier drafts; state the positive fact in each case:
   - "rather than evidence that this cap is generally a good compromise"
   - "not optimised to the observed results"
   - "not a claim that people know their accuracy"
   - "Rounded endpoints do not imply exact certainty"
8. **Repeated sentences.**
   - The exact contrasts are given in two stand-alone sentences and then again in Table 1. Keep the table.
   - The superior/inferior/tied grid counts and the four sign-pattern ranges use template sentences. Put them in two small tables.
   - In the Introduction, "Its narrower target … contribute exactly zero" repeats the preceding paragraph.
9. **Table 2.** The "Selection" column mixes "random" with ratio values. Use labels such as "all cases" and "disagreements, k = 1/4/8".
10. **Byline.** The byline names the intended author, but they have not endorsed the draft. Add a draft notice such as "Author approval pending" beside the title. This matches the disclosure and is not a certification.
11. **Future submission requirements.** Record these as open items, not current deficiencies: human accountability and sign-off, funding and competing-interest declarations, and public archiving with a DOI. The limitation of the git preregistration (commit 68c07e4) is stated accurately and should stay.
12. **References.** The rendered bibliography is complete for the in-text citations. I have not independently checked metadata or DOIs and make no claims about them. The response log's account of the Crossref and DataCite checks is plausible.

## Answer

**Verdict: minor-to-moderate revision (short of a full major revision).** Yes, substantive major issues remain. Three do:

1. The paper does not consider that the verification ratio, or a post-stratified correction, can be estimated from observed disagreements, so the "known weights" caveat and the misspecification framing are overstated.
2. The manuscript does not state that the registered grid covers only the δ_L > 0 ≥ δ_H crossing direction, so the grid counts appear broader than they are.
3. The proposed experiment replaces the participant's own judgements with a fixed history and does not specify the absolute account's benchmark, which weakens its refutability for the self-versus-advisor question.

None of these undermines the registered numerical contrasts. Their directions are verified by exact convolution and are properly framed as model consequences, not human evidence. The global-adoption scope is justified well enough. Issues 2 and 3 can be fixed with text. Issue 1 needs text, and ideally one small exact exploratory check. With these fixes and the minor items, the manuscript should be acceptable in round 3. Before any submission, it still needs human endorsement, accountability declarations and public archiving.
