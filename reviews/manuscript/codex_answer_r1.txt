## Answer

1. **MAJOR — H3 decision rule and Table 3.** M1R is retained even though the observed curvature *g* falls outside its predictive interval. It survives because the alternative statistic ρ = *g*/*b* has a very wide interval when simulated *b* is close to zero. Thus “retained” does not establish a good fit to the observed response curve. Report a joint predictive check for *b* and *g*, or a check of the response function itself, and describe M1R as inconclusive under the current rule.

2. **MAJOR — Across-cell analysis and model adequacy.** The slope *d* compares different items and studies whose anchors were chosen by researchers. Extremity is therefore confounded with item knowledge, anchor choice and design. Treating failure to predict *d* as a decisive failure of a within-item process model is too strong. Keep H2 as a descriptive test of generalisation, and distinguish its evidence from the within-item model test in Table 3 and the conclusions.

3. **MAJOR — H2 standard errors.** The method says that each index’s standard error is derived from condition MADs, but does not give the formula or explain how it handles participants who answered under both anchor conditions. Such responses yield correlated condition medians; ignoring that covariance can change weights and uncertainty. Provide the formula, identify paired designs, and repeat H2 with participant-level resampling or another method that preserves each study’s design.

4. **MAJOR — Scope of the primary inference.** Bahník (2021) supplies 83% of estimates and 13 of 20 cells. Removing it leaves a curvature interval spanning both substantial decline and increase. The pooled result is convincing for the represented random-anchor tasks, but the manuscript’s broader claims about general-knowledge magnitudes and process accounts exceed the independent evidence. Make this scope explicit in the abstract and conclusion, and report study-specific estimates alongside the pooled estimate.

5. **MAJOR — Process-model framing.** M2 is a constant-weight integration model **on the log scale**; a constant weight on raw magnitudes would not generally produce constant log elasticity. Likewise, M3 and M4 are particular formal versions of plausibility accounts, not unique predictions of Epley and Gilovich or selective accessibility. State these modelling choices where the models are introduced and frame the rejections as applying to the specified implementations, rather than to the wider theories.

6. **MAJOR — Predictive intervals and calibration.** The intervals depend on the accepted parameter grid, its implicit weighting, a broad calibration range, and assumptions about belief uncertainty and response errors. The relaxed-calibration exercise draws only 300 parameter sets per model, so finding no joint match is not evidence that none exists. Specify how accepted parameters were sampled, show sensitivity to the parameter ranges and weighting, and qualify claims that M3 and M4 predict a decline that is “too steep.”

7. **MINOR — Measures and interpretation of H1.** *b* is the fitted elasticity **at** the true value, not the “average elasticity at the true value.” Local elasticity is also distinct from the share of the full anchor–belief gap carried into an estimate. Define both quantities precisely and use “local elasticity” for the reported values 0.31, 0.23 and 0.14.

8. **MINOR — H1 robustness.** The random-anchor fallback fixes between-cell curvature variance at zero, making its interval especially narrow. The separate near/far analysis reports only a slope difference, so readers cannot tell whether far-anchor slopes remain positive. Report both slopes with intervals, and give an uncertainty check that preserves participant clustering and allows curvature to vary across items.

9. **MINOR — Preregistration and deviations.** The deviations file is unusually clear, but the manuscript should disclose that the reported operating characteristics approximate the gates and use the full random-effects structure even where the observed stratified fits required a fallback. It should also identify the MAD floor and zero-SE exclusion as safeguards present in the preregistered code but absent from its prose. Provide the repository link and frozen preregistration for review.

10. **MINOR — Unit mismatch and data accounting.** The 870 Chicago responses appear to be valid answers in millions, rather than gross errors. State that the entire cell was excluded because its units were inconsistent, and, if the intended units can be established, show a sensitivity analysis after conversion. Clarify that 7,952 and 129 are eligible-design counts, whereas 7,949 and 128 are analysed counts.

11. **MINOR — Presentation and references.** Figure 3 calls 1/SE “precision” although the discussed inverse-variance weight is 1/SE². Correct the caption and repair visible reference-formatting errors, including the Lieder et al. author entry and literal HTML tags in several titles.

**Overall recommendation: major revision.** The within-item negative curvature is a substantial finding, and the inconclusive across-cell result is reported candidly. **Major issues remain** in the model-survival rule, the interpretation of the across-cell test, the H2 uncertainty estimates, and the breadth of the theoretical conclusions.
